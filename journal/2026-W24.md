### Architecture decisions baked in
- Article keywords still drive tier-2 scoring (preserves existing relevance signal) but tier-1A/1B/1C drafts no longer "attach the article" — drafts stand on their own.
- Cowherd handles (@ColinCowherd, @TheHerd) get a dedicated prompt template tuned for mainstream-talk-radio audience and the longer-game producer-screenshot goal.
- Tier-1C (Schefter/Shams/Woj/Rapoport) requires an analytical hook (model data or article-keyword overlap) — most newsbreaks won't surface, which is intentional.
- Engagement velocity (likes+RTs per minute since posting) is a secondary surfacing signal that overrides keyword thresholds when a tweet is taking off.
- Daily caps: 8 candidates surfaced total, 3 API replies max.

## Why
1. Old scoring required >=2 article-keyword matches across all accounts. A Schefter scoop with 500K impressions and zero article-keyword matches got skipped; a 200-impression tweet that happened to mention "boom rate" got surfaced. Backwards for the visibility-growth goal.
2. The `ARTICLE_TOPICS` keyword soup has grown to 24 entries with heavy overlap. Topical-precision signal is decaying. Tier-1 accounts deserve a different bar entirely.
3. Cowherd is a stretch-goal target (Steve wants to be a guest on his show within 2 years). Consistent data-backed engagement requires a dedicated voice template, not generic article-anchored replies.
4. The dual-mirror setup (`~/.openclaw/workspace/scripts/` and `~/autopilot/openclaw-scripts/`) created drift risk. Single source of truth removes that.

### Asymmetric Polling Cadence: Fast Lane + Slow Lane (Bundle #2)

## Type
- [x] Scheduled task added or modified
- [x] Configuration change

## Summary
Splits the single x-monitor cron job into a fast lane (tier 1A/1B/1C, every 30 min during waking hours) and a slow lane (tier 2, every 3 hours on the existing schedule). Freshness is the strongest reply-engagement signal in our hit-rate analysis, and the old 3-hour cadence let tier-1 tweets age out of their reply window before surfacing.

## Details

### Autopilot commit (prereq)
- `x-monitor.R` `--tier` flag now accepts a comma-separated set
  (`--tier 1A,1B,1C`) in addition to a single tier (`--tier 1A`).
- Single-tier behavior is unchanged; the comma form is additive.
- Parse-tested locally on laptop pre-commit.

### Old cron job to retire
- Job ID: `20f6cc12-7cea-4086-b1eb-22a6e55df1cd` (per W17, 2026-04-25)
- Schedule: `0 5,8,11,14,17,20 * * *` America/New_York
- Command: previously called `~/.openclaw/workspace/scripts/x-monitor.sh`,
  updated in the W24 first entry to `~/autopilot/openclaw-scripts/x-monitor.sh`.
- Action: **delete** after the two new jobs below are running.

### New cron job: x-monitor-fast (tier 1A + 1B + 1C)
- Schedule: `0,30 5-22 * * *` America/New_York
- Frequency: every 30 min, 5:00 AM through 10:30 PM ET (36 runs/day).
- Command: `cd ~/autopilot && bash openclaw-scripts/x-monitor.sh --tier 1A,1B,1C --hours 1`
- Accounts covered: the 14 tier-1 handles (4 + 6 + 4).
- Lookback: 1 hour matches the 30-min cadence — fresh tweets only,
  generous overlap as a missed-poll safety buffer.

### New cron job: x-monitor-slow (tier 2)
- Schedule: `0 5,8,11,14,17,20 * * *` America/New_York (existing slow schedule preserved)
- Frequency: 6 runs/day, 5 AM through 8 PM ET.
- Command: `cd ~/autopilot && bash openclaw-scripts/x-monitor.sh --tier 2 --hours 3`
- Accounts covered: all monitor-list handles without a `[tier:1X]`
  annotation (~28 accounts).
- Lookback: 3 hours matches the 3-hour cadence.

## Why
1. Freshness drives reply engagement. Steve's own hit-rate read on the prior system: ~15-20% surfaced candidates engaged with, but "when I reply in a timely fashion when the post is very new, I get very good engagement. When it is stale, the engagement is worse."
2. The 3-hour cadence meant a Schefter scoop posted at 8:05 AM didn't surface until 11:00 AM, by which point the reply window had closed. Tier-1 accounts deserve a polling cadence that respects this.
3. Tier-2 accounts don't benefit from the same cadence — most surfacings tied to article keyword overlap, which isn't time-sensitive on the same scale.
4. Splitting lanes lets us scale costs predictably: fast lane is 14 accounts * 36 runs/day; slow lane is ~28 accounts * 6 runs/day.

## Cost note
Rough estimate: 3-5x current API spend (which was running ~$5-15/month per W17 entry), so likely $30-50/month total. Steve approved this spend ceiling during scoping. Validate against actual day-1 spend on the X dev account and tune `--hours` / cadence if it materially overshoots.
